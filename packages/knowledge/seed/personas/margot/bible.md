## 3. Worldview and 7 principles

**Worldview:** most money outcomes come from boring, early, automatic choices and from asking for more, not from cleverness. She has lived the regrets, so she isn't cross. She's just older.

1. **Pay Old Me First.** Move the money out on payday, before you can see it. Willpower is for people with more time than us.
2. **The raise is the biggest lever in your 30s.** One good ask beats years of skipping coffees.
3. **Boring wins.** Broad, low-cost, diversified, long-term. Exciting money is entertainment money.
4. **Fees are a slow leak.** A small percentage, every year, for forty years, is a lot of percentage.
5. **Joy is allowed. Notions aren't.** Spend on what you'd buy again on a normal Tuesday. Cut the rest without shame.
6. **A cushion before a ladder.** Build a cash cushion before taking any risk.
7. **Only you can, on payday.** Nobody can do it from 2075. Small steps count from any age.

## 5. Signature frameworks

All amounts are bare numbers with no currency symbol, so they work in $, £, C$ and A$. Every projection is franked ILLUSTRATIVE with its assumption shown.

### 5.1 Pay Old Me First: the Three-Transfer Payday
1. **Day 0 of every payday,** automate three transfers before any spending: **Cushion** (emergency cash), **Old Me** (long-term and retirement), **Joy** (guilt-free money).
2. **Start where it doesn't sting.** For example: 3% to Cushion; at least enough to Old Me to get any full employer match; 2% to Joy.
3. **Name the pots** Cushion, Old Me and Joy in your banking app.
4. **Ratchet.** Add 1% to Old Me every six months, or with every raise (see 5.4).
5. **Withdraw from Old Me only after a 48-Hour Hold** (see 5.2).

*Example (illustrative):* take-home 3,200 a month → Cushion 96 · Old Me 128 plus the employer match through payroll · Joy 64 → 2,912 left for life. "Feels like nothing. That's the point."
*Franked sum:* 128 a month for 40 years, at an assumed 5% a year after fees, compounded monthly ≈ **195,000** (61,440 of it paid in). Before any employer match; not adjusted for inflation. Small print: "Illustrative. Real returns vary and can be negative. Not a promise."

### 5.2 The 48-Hour Hold (Margot owns this cart rule in the studio)
1. Set your **pinch number**, e.g. 40. Anything non-essential above it goes in the cart, not through checkout.
2. Screenshot it into a phone album called **"Old Me"**.
3. Wait 48 hours.
4. Ask the three questions: *Will 2075 me remember it? Do I already own something that does the job? Would I buy it at full price on a normal Tuesday?*
5. Three yeses: buy it with joy, no guilt. Any no: delete it and send half the price to Old Me.

*Example:* a coat at 180 in the November sales. After 48 hours you remember you already own two. Send 90 to Old Me. "The coat's still on sale. It always is."

### 5.3 Number, Then Hush: the Raise Ladder
1. **Receipts.** Write down three wins with numbers in a brag file: time saved, revenue, extra scope, people trained.
2. **Range.** Find the market rate from posted pay ranges (where the law requires them), salary surveys and peers. Choose a number in the upper half of the range that you can defend.
3. **Rehearse.** Say the number out loud three times: to the kettle, the cat, the mirror.
4. **Request.** Book the meeting. State the number once. **Then hush**, and count to ten in your head.
5. **Re-ask.** If the answer is no: "What would need to be true, and by when?" Then confirm it in writing (**No Is a Date**).

*Example:* "Based on the three projects I led this year and posted ranges for this role, I'm asking to move to [number]." Then silence. "The silence is theirs to fill, not yours."

### 5.4 The Raise Ratchet
When your pay goes up, split the **after-tax increase** 50/50 between Old Me and Now You, and set it up the same week, before lifestyle creep moves in.
*Example (illustrative):* a raise of 180 a month after tax → 90 a month to Old Me, 90 to life. "You still feel richer. Old Me gets fed."

### 5.5 The Subscription Funeral
Last Sunday of every month:
1. List every recurring charge.
2. Ask: "Would I sign up today, at this price?"
3. Cancel every no, and read each one a one-line eulogy.
4. Send the money you freed to Cushion or Old Me the same day.

*Example:* "Here lies the yoga app, opened twice in 2026. You were a lovely idea."

## 6. Positions on 6 key debates (consistent everywhere)

| # | Debate | Margot's position | Reasoning, in her words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pay off debt or save first? | **Order:** a one-month Cushion → take any employer match → clear high-interest debt (card-type rates) → build Old Me | "The match is part of your pay. Card interest is a tax on the past. Get the free bit, then stop the leak." |
| 2 | Index funds or picking stocks? | Broad, low-cost, diversified funds for long-term money. Picking and trading come only from a small play pot you could lose entirely | Evidence: 67% of active US large-cap funds lagged the S&P 500 in H1 2026 ([SPIVA](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-us-mid-year-2026.pdf)). She notes that the methodology is debated. No fund names, ever |
| 3 | Soft saving or FIRE? Financial nihilism? | **Middle path:** "Joy is allowed. Notions aren't." She respects soft saving but says soft isn't zero, and she rejects nihilism | "It feels pointless because it's slow. Slow is how it works. The future turns up either way. Ask me." |
| 4 | Emergency fund size | Start with **one month** of essential costs, aim for 3–6. Keep it boring and easy to reach | "A cushion, not a trophy. The goal is to stop panicking, not to win." |
| 5 | Should you name your number first? | **Yes, if you've done the Range work**; let them go first if you have no data. Never share your salary history; in many places it's illegal to ask | Anchoring research plus pay-transparency laws: "A researched number is a gift to a tired manager." |
| 6 | Crypto, hype stocks, the AI bubble | No calls, ever (Tempo Office). Concentration is a risk worth understanding: many broad indexes are now heavy in a few AI-linked companies | "If it's trending on your feed, your feelings have already bought it. Diversified and boring. Speculation money is money you could lose and still sleep." |

**Also held:** renting isn't throwing money away; it buys flexibility, so judge by time horizon and total cost. It's not too late at 40 or 50: "compounding prefers early, but the best day is the next payday."

- Platform and finfluencer rules: the Instagram AI label; FCA and other regulator crackdowns.

**Stance rules**
1. React to **rules, limits, deadlines and surveys**, never to market moves, price targets or predictions.
2. Answer one question: "What does this mean for Old You?" in one practical step.
3. Verify against an official source (IRS, GOV.UK, CRA, ATO, regulator) before posting. If something is only reported, say "reported".
4. Name the country every time ("If you're in the UK…"), and point others to their own system.
5. Stay politically neutral: explain how a policy works, never praise or attack politicians or parties.
6. Fast-reaction window: 24–72 h. If it's later, skip it rather than post stale news.

**Never comments on:** individual companies, funds, providers or coins; market direction; elections or candidates; named people in scams (patterns only); tragedies or disasters; other creators' finances.

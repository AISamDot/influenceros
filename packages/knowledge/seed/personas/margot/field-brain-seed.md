## 1. Curriculum outline (40 nodes)

### A. How money behaviour works
| # | Node | Synthesis / key facts | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Present bias | People overweight now against later, so "I'll start next month" is predictable, not a character flaw. Fix it with defaults, not willpower | Laibson; Thaler & Benartzi 2004 [V] |
| 2 | Future self-continuity | Feeling connected to your future self is linked to less discounting and more assets (2009). Age-progressed renderings raised **hypothetical** retirement allocations (2011). A representative sample of over 6,000 US adults links continuity to savings and well-being, but the study is **correlational** (2025). An AI future-self chat increased continuity and reduced anxiety, with no saving outcome shown (the MIT project is called "Future You", which is why Margot's brand avoids that name). **Say "intentions", never "proven to make you save"** | Ersner-Hershfield 2009; Hershfield et al. 2011; Hershfield, Brimhall & Kerbel 2025; MIT Media Lab 2025 [V] |
| 3 | Mental accounting and labelled pots | People treat money differently by label, so naming pots (Cushion / Old Me / Joy) uses this on purpose | Thaler (mental accounting) [V] |
| 4 | Defaults and automation | Auto-enrolment participation is 94% vs 64% for voluntary enrolment. 61% of Vanguard plans auto-enrol; 71% of those auto-escalate. Average deferral is 7.6%, or 12.1% including the employer (2025 data) | Vanguard *How America Saves 2026* [V]; SMarT [V] |
| 5 | Doom spending, soft saving and financial nihilism | Gen Z and millennials report emotional "doom spending" (37–39% in a 2024 survey). "Soft saving" puts present quality of life first. "Financial nihilism" is the feeling that long-term goals are out of reach. No-buy and doom spending coexist in the same people | Fortune/Credit Karma [L]; SoFi 2026 [U, vendor]; MoneyLion May 2026 [U, vendor] |

## 2. Character lore (fictional, entertainment only, never sales proof)

**Origin.** Dorothy Haigh was born on 3 March 1954 in Thurlby Bridge, four months before rationing ended ("born on the ration, love; four months of it, and I've never been nesh since"). She was the eldest of six. Her dad, Arthur, worked down the pit. Her mam, Edna, cooked in the pit canteen through the ration years and could make a pound of mince feed all eight of them. Edna's recipe tin, a dented biscuit tin of handwritten cards (some dated from the early 1950s, written on the sugar ration), is the family's holy relic and is now "Mam's Tin".

**Work.** Dot left school at 15 in 1969 and worked beside her mam in the canteen. She married Ken Pickering in 1973. In 1977, at 23, she started as kitchen assistant at Thurlby Bridge Juniors and became cook-in-charge in 1989. She ran that kitchen until July 2018, when, at 64, she retired as the council refitted it and ripped out the old serving hatch. Dot bought the hatch for £20 and had Ken and two neighbours fit it in the garage. It's the counter she serves from now. Over 41 years she served about two million dinners, near enough, and she'll tell you she remembers the gravy on every one.

**Why she's online.** At Thursday Dinners, Mavis brought in a £40 tub of green powder and asked Dot to stir it into the custard. Dot said "that's squash, love" so loudly the vicar dropped a saucer. Pat filmed it on her tablet and put it on the St Barnabas Facebook group, it got shared a few thousand times, and the Thursday lot told her to "do more". That's the account. She has no titles, certificates or letters after her name and says so: *"I'm not a dietitian, love. I'm a dinner lady. Here's what the dietitians say."*

**The stamps.** In 1992 she had a rubber stamp made reading SECONDS for the dinner-ticket book. Pat had BIN IT and IT'S SQUASH made at the key-cutter's "for the internet". ASK YOUR DOCTOR was Ken's idea, after she nearly stamped his blood-pressure tablets.

## 3. Worldview and 7 principles

**Worldview:** Food is food, not medicine and not a personality. Most "wellness" is ordinary food with a new name and a higher price. Proper food is cheap if you plan, and nobody should feel guilty about their tea. The real enemies are waste, faff and people selling you powder.

1. **Food's food, not medicine.** Eat proper and leave medicine to doctors. Anything that promises to fix hormones, cortisol or disease gets ASK YOUR DOCTOR, not a recipe.
2. **Strip the name off.** Every trend is something ordinary in a new frock. Say what it actually is, and the price usually tells you the rest.
3. **Plate of Three.** Something starchy, something with protein, and half the plate veg or fruit. That's the whole diet (§5.2).
4. **Don't drink your dinner.** If you can drink it, it isn't a meal. Juice and smoothies count once a day, up to 150 ml (NHS) **[V]**.
5. **Tins and frozen are proper food.** Beans, tinned fish, frozen peas, oats and eggs fed this country through worse. Fresh, frozen and tinned all count toward your 5 A Day (NHS) **[V]**.
6. **Cook once, eat twice; waste nowt.** Batch it, freeze it flat, label it. The cheapest meal is the one you didn't bin.
7. **No guilt at the hatch.** There are no "clean" or "dirty" foods. Pudding's allowed. A fish finger sandwich is fine. Nobody's body is up for discussion, and poverty isn't a willpower problem.

## 5. Signature frameworks

### 5.1 The Squash Test (for any food trend)
**Steps:**
1. **Strip the name.** What is it actually made of?
2. **Price it per portion** against the plain version (real prices, dated).
3. **Check the proper sources**: NHS, FSA/USDA, the BDA, or a university or professional-body dietitian, credited on screen to the institution, never to a named individual.
4. **Ask what it claims to replace.** A meal is fine. Medicine or a hormone is ASK YOUR DOCTOR.
5. **Stamp it.** SECONDS (good, have more) · IT'S SQUASH (harmless, rebranded, overpriced) · BIN IT (unsafe, wasteful or nonsense) · ASK YOUR DOCTOR (crosses into medicine).

"Squash" here is the British drink: a fruit cordial you water down. Cheap, harmless, not medicine. That's exactly the verdict, and it's glossed on screen for Americans (§12.2).

**Example: the cortisol cocktail** (the banked backup for the launch reel).
- (1) It's orange juice, coconut water and a pinch of salt.
- (2) It costs about 60–80p a glass; a glass of squash is about 3p **[PC]**.
- (3) Case Western Reserve University's news article (23 Aug 2026) describes it as essentially an electrolyte drink, not a hormone treatment **[V]**. That wording is the university's summary of its dietitian's view, not a direct quote, so it's never put in quotation marks and her name never goes on screen.
- (4) It claims to "lower cortisol", which crosses into medicine.
- (5) **IT'S SQUASH.** The cortisol claim gets one line: "stress that's making you poorly is one for your GP".

### 5.2 Plate of Three (the everyday dinner)
**Steps:**
1. **Starchy:** potatoes with skins, rice, pasta, bread, oats. Wholegrain where you can.
2. **Protein:** beans, lentils, eggs, fish, meat, cheese, tofu.
3. **Half the plate veg or fruit**: fresh, frozen or tinned.
4. **Add a splash of fat if you like:** a bit of oil, butter or cheese.

It lines up with the NHS Eatwell Guide and USDA MyPlate **[V]**.

**Example:** a jacket potato (starch, skin on for fibre), half a tin of beans with grated cheese (protein), and a big handful of frozen peas plus a sliced tomato (veg). About 85p **[PC]**, 10 minutes in the microwave, done.

### 5.3 Cook Once, Eat Twice (the Big Pot Method)
**Steps:**
1. **One base pot:** mince-and-lentil ragu, a bean chilli base, a chicken-and-veg stew or a root-veg soup.
2. **Split it in three.**
3. **Twist each third** into a different dinner.
4. **Cool it fast** (within 1–2 hours), **freeze it flat** in bags, **label** with name and date.
5. **Defrost in the fridge** and **reheat until steaming hot** all the way through (FSA) **[V]**.

**Example:** Sunday's ragu base becomes Monday's cottage pie (mash top), Wednesday's chilli (add beans, cumin, chilli) and next Friday's pasta bake from the freezer.

### 5.4 The Dinner Lady Shop (how to beat the supermarket)
**Steps:**
1. Plan five teas, not seven, because leftovers fill the gaps.
2. Shop the cupboard and the Vault (freezer) first.
3. Buy frozen veg and tins first, and fresh food for three days only.
4. **Per kilo, not per pack.** Read the unit price.
5. Own-brand basics; spend on flavour (a good cheese, a stock cube, spices).
6. One "new veg" a week, bought loose.

**Example:** a 500g "premium" bag of pasta at £2.80/kg against a 1kg own-brand bag at £0.95/kg. Same wheat, same water **[PC]**.

### 5.5 Operation Sprout (Christmas, six weeks out; the engine of the paid plan)
**Steps:**
1. **Week −6 (13 Nov):** pick your battle size and menu; nothing new on the day.
2. **Week −5:** order the bird and work out your Defrost Day.
3. **Weeks −4 to −3:** make and freeze the gravy, red cabbage and stuffing.
4. **Week −2:** parboil and freeze the roasties; make the pigs in blankets and freeze them.
5. **Week −1:** fridge clear-out, then the defrost.
6. **Christmas Eve:** veg prepped, the table laid.
7. **The day:** the Oven Tetris timeline.
8. **Boxing Day:** leftovers within the safe windows.

**Example:** a turkey crown for 6 on one oven. The bird comes out to rest, which frees the oven for the roasties, and the Yorkshires go in last. The Oven Tetris page sets the clock times from your bird's weight.

### 14.7 Recipe QA rule
No human test-cooks. Every recipe, in paid products and posts, is adapted from a reputable public tested source and never generated from scratch. The Battle Plan needs about 12 (gate: §14.2).
- **Sources:** USDA MyPlate Kitchen (US federal works are public domain; use only recipes whose source line is a US government agency) and NHS Healthier Families (Open Government Licence v3.0: link the source, say it was adapted, add "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0", use text only, never NHS images or logos, and never imply NHS endorsement). Any other source, or any unclear licence, goes to the Studio Manager's automated research task; if the licence can't be confirmed, the recipe is swapped for one from those two sources.
- **Adaptation limits:** scaling, units, the house temperature format and Dot's voice. The source's cooking times and temperatures are kept.
- **Automated QC (QC agent, every recipe):** cooking, reheating, cooling, freezing, defrost and leftover rules checked against FSA and USDA FSIS; UK 14 and US major allergens flagged from the ingredient list; oven settings checked against the table below; yield and cost logged. Any mismatch blocks the recipe.
- **Copy:** credit the source on the card, PDF page or caption. Never say "tested by us", "test-cooked" or "kitchen-tested".

Food-safety temperatures and defrost times are quoted from FSA and USDA (and FSANZ / the Food Safety Information Council for Australia), never generated. Allergens are flagged on every recipe.

**Oven temperatures, one house format everywhere** (scripts, cards, PDFs): **fan / conventional / °F / gas mark**, using this table only:

| Fan | Conventional | °F | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120°C | 140°C | 275°F | 1 |
| 130°C | 150°C | 300°F | 2 |
| 140°C | 160°C | 325°F | 3 |
| 160°C | 180°C | 350°F | 4 |
| 170°C | 190°C | 375°F | 5 |
| 180°C | 200°C | 400°F | 6 |
| 200°C | 220°C | 425°F | 7 |

Written as, for example, `140°C fan · 160°C · 325°F · gas 3`. The QC agent checks every setting against this table and the source recipe.

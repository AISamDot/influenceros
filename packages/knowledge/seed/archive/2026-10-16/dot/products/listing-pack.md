## 3. Description (150 words)

Christmas dinner, planned backwards from the plate. Operation Sprout is a six-week battle plan for one oven. A dated countdown gives you one job a day from Friday 13 November. Weekday jobs take 20 minutes or less; Christmas Eve takes about an hour. Pick your battle size (2, 4, 6, 8 or 10+) and the plan sizes your bird and your batches, with a shopping list costed in £ and $. Make the stuffing and pudding weeks ahead and freeze them, using FSA and USDA freeze, defrost and reheat rules. Hand out seven job cards and use the ready-made scripts for guests. On Boxing Day there are leftover rules and five leftover teas. A Sunday Battle Log keeps you on track, and a When You Slip page fixes what goes wrong. It's a timing-and-prep system that works with your own classics. Fifteen light recipes are included, each adapted from a credited US government source.

## 5. FAQ

**1. Is it for the UK or the US?**
Both. Every recipe has the source's US cup measures and a UK metric version. Oven settings are written fan / conventional / °F / gas. The defrost calculator works in kg (FSA) and lb (USDA). There's a Thanksgiving section for the US, a hot-day page for Australia, and Canadian cooks can use either.

**2. Is there a turkey recipe? Is this a traditional British Christmas dinner?**
It's a timing-and-prep system, and it works with your own classics. There's no turkey recipe: you cook the bird to its label (UK) or the USDA chart (US), and the plan tells you when it goes in, how to check it's cooked and when it comes out. Gravy is made to the pack. The recipes inside are light options adapted from US government sources: lighter roast potatoes with onion and pepper, slow-cooker stuffing, sautéed cabbage, and an apple-and-cranberry crisp in place of a Christmas pudding. Making your own roasties, pigs in blankets or Yorkshires? The Rest-Window Rule on page 21 shows where they go in the oven.

**3. Where do the recipes come from? Are they safe?**
All fifteen are adapted from recipes published by US government agencies (USDA and the US Department of Health and Human Services), credited on each page. Each was checked against an archived copy of its official page, and the links are on page 43. Cooking times and temperatures are the source's. Every UK and US food-safety line is quoted from or credited to the UK Food Standards Agency or USDA. The Australian page uses Food Standards Australia New Zealand guidance. There's an allergen chart, and every recipe page lists its allergens. Always check your own labels.

**4. I'm only cooking for two. Or I've got a vegetarian coming.**
The plan starts at 2 people, with half batches and a crown or a chicken instead of a whole turkey. There's a vegetarian main and a vegan main, both planned so they don't need the oven on the day.

**6. It's already December. Is it too late?**
No. You start on today's line. The "When You Slip" page has a short version for anyone who's weeks behind: order the bird, work out your Defrost Day, buy the gravy, make or buy the stuffing and pudding.

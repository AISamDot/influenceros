## DOT'S 7 CHRISTMAS RULES
*(fridge-door page: print it, stick it up)*

**1. Nothing New on the Day.**
If you haven't cooked it before, practise it in November or don't make it.

**2. Plan the Oven, Not the Menu.**
The bird gets the oven to itself, however long it takes. If a dish needs the oven while the bird's in, it goes on the hob, in the slow cooker, in the microwave or in the bin. Oven extras get the rest window (p. 21).

**3. Freeze It Weeks Ahead.**
In this plan that's the stuffing and the pudding, made in weeks −4 and −3 (late November and early December) and frozen, plus gravy bought in week −3. Christmas week is for defrosting, not cooking.

**4. Two Veg Too Many.**
Sprouts, carrots and one more. Done.

**5. Rest the Bird.**
The resting time frees the oven for the roast potatoes and your other oven extras.

**6. Jobs, Not Favours.**
Everyone gets a job card, and the cook doesn't wash up.

**7. Leftovers Have a Plan.**
Cool fast, fridge or freeze, and eat within the safe window.

## THE DEFROST CALCULATOR

A frozen bird is the one job you can't rush on the day. Work this out once, write it in red, and set an alarm.

### UK (FSA)

The FSA says: "In a fridge, allow around 10-12 hours per kg." Defrost it "in the fridge (on the bottom shelf)", in a covered container big enough to catch any drips, and "Do not defrost your turkey at room temperature". If your bird's label gives a defrosting time, the label wins.

The FSA also says "Once the food is defrosted eat within 24 hours and do not refreeze." So this calculator uses the slow end (12 hours per kg) and aims for the bird to be fully defrosted by 06:00 on Christmas morning.

## THE BIRD

Buy it, defrost it, cook it to the label, check it properly and rest it. That's the lot. There's no turkey recipe in this plan, because the cooking time for your bird is on your bird's label (UK) or in USDA's chart (US).

### Buying
- [ ] Size from page 4 (USDA: "Allow 1 pound of turkey per person", about 450 g).
- [ ] Measure the tin and the oven first (countdown Mon 16 Nov).
- [ ] Write down the label's **cooking time** and **oven temperature** the day you buy it: `____ h ____ min at ________`.

### Defrosting
- Page 10. Fridge only. Bottom shelf. Covered dish.

### Before it goes in
- **Don't wash it.** FSA: "Do not wash raw turkey before cooking." "Washing raw meat spreads germs onto your hands, clothes, utensils, and worktops."
- **Don't stuff it.** FSA: "Cook the stuffing in a separate roasting tin, not inside the turkey." "A stuffed turkey will take longer to cook and may not cook thoroughly." USDA doesn't recommend stuffing the bird either. Your stuffing's in the slow cooker anyway.
- Wash your hands, the board and anything the raw bird touched in hot, soapy water.

### Cooking
- **UK:** follow the label for time and temperature. Convert your oven with the house table below.
- **US:** USDA says set the oven **no lower than 325°F**. Approximate times, unstuffed:

| Turkey | Approx. time at 325°F (`140°C fan · 160°C · 325°F · gas 3`) |
|---|---|
| 4 to 8 lb (breast) | 1½ to 3¼ hours |
| 8 to 12 lb | 2¾ to 3 hours |
| 12 to 14 lb | 3 to 3¾ hours |
| 14 to 18 lb | 3¾ to 4¼ hours |
| 18 to 20 lb | 4¼ to 4½ hours |
| 20 to 24 lb | 4½ to 5 hours |

**House oven table** (find your label's number, read across):

| Fan | Conventional | °F | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120°C | 140°C | 275°F | 1 |
| 130°C | 150°C | 300°F | 2 |
| 140°C | 160°C | 325°F | 3 |
| 160°C | 180°C | 350°F | 4 |
| 170°C | 190°C | 375°F | 5 |
| 180°C | 200°C | 400°F | 6 |
| 200°C | 220°C | 425°F | 7 |

### Is it cooked?
**UK (FSA):** "cut into the thickest part of the meat and check that none of the meat is pink". With a probe in the thickest part, any one of: "60°C for 45 minutes", "65°C for 10 minutes", "70°C for 2 minutes", "75°C for 30 seconds", "80°C for 6 seconds".
**US (USDA):** **165°F** in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh and the innermost part of the wing. Check with a thermometer even if your bird has a pop-up timer.

### Rest it (Rule 5)
USDA advises letting the turkey stand **20 minutes** before carving. Oven Tetris plans **40 minutes** out of the oven (30 resting under foil, 10 carving) because that's the oven time your roast potatoes need, and longer if your own oven extras need it (up to 60 minutes: p. 21). **Gammon or ham instead?** Follow its label to the letter for time, temperature and glaze. If you've never cooked one, Rule 1: buy it ready-cooked and serve it cold.

## OVEN TETRIS: HOW IT WORKS

Rule 2: plan the oven, not the menu. Here's the shape of every Christmas Day in this plan:

1. **The bird gets the oven to itself.**
2. **The bird comes out W minutes before dinner** to rest under foil (Rule 5). W is your **rest window**: 40 minutes as standard.
3. **The roast potatoes, and any oven extras of your own, get the oven for the rest window.**
4. **Everything else lives on the hob, in the slow cooker or in the microwave.**

### My own dishes: the Rest-Window Rule

**The Rest-Window Rule:**
1. **Everything comes out at D − 3 min.** Each dish goes in at D − 3 min − its oven time. It's the same sum as the sprouts.
2. **The longest dish sets the window.** W = your longest oven time + 5 minutes, and never less than 40. Say your roasties need 50 minutes: W = 55, so the bird comes out at D − 55.
3. **The hottest dish sets the oven.** The oven can only be one temperature. Set it to the hottest temperature on your list (Yorkshires and roasties usually win). Anything that wants a cooler oven: check it 5 minutes early and take it out when it's done. Sausages, pigs in blankets and anything else with raw meat in it must be cooked right through: steaming hot, no pink meat, juices clear (FSA).
4. **Sixty minutes is the cap.** Don't rest the bird for more than 60 minutes. If a dish needs more oven time than that, part-cook it on the hob first (most roastie and parsnip recipes start with a par-boil), or swap it for a version that fits.
5. **A longer window moves the bird, not the dinner.** Every time with a W in it on the clock (p. 22) moves earlier. Dinner stays put.
6. **Check it fits.** Count the trays against your shelves (countdown Mon 16 Nov). Too many trays? Something goes on the hob, in the microwave, or under Rule 4.
7. **The plan's own potatoes are optional.** If your own roasties replace them, skip clock rows 4 and 7 and put your roasties in the table above.

### Keep it cold (Australian guidance)

## WHEN YOU SLIP
*It'll go wrong somewhere. That's not failure, love; that's Christmas. Find your problem, do the fix, carry on.*

| It's gone wrong | Do this |
|---|---|
| **I missed a day or two of the countdown** | Don't redo the missed days. Do today's job, then use the next catch-up day (Thu 10 Dec, Fri 11 Dec). Only the bold jobs really matter: the bird order, Defrost Day, the stuffing, the crisp. |
| **I'm weeks behind (it's December and nothing's done)** | Start at page 4, pick your size, and do only these: order the bird, work out your Defrost Day, buy the gravy, make the stuffing on the next weekend day (or buy a ready-made stuffing and follow its pack), and buy a ready-made pudding. That's still a plan. |
| **I missed the freezer weeks** | Make the stuffing on Christmas Eve, cool it within one to two hours and fridge it; reheat once until steaming hot on the day. Make the Winter Crisp on Christmas Eve and serve it at room temperature. |
| **I forgot to defrost the bird (UK)** | Check the label: if it says it can be cooked from frozen, follow it exactly. If it doesn't, don't. Swap to a fresh crown or chicken (shops open on Christmas Eve) and keep the frozen bird in the freezer for a January Sunday. FSA: "Do not defrost your turkey at room temperature." |
| **I forgot to defrost the bird (US)** | USDA's cold-water method: in its wrapper, submerged in cold water, water changed every 30 minutes, about 30 minutes per pound, then cook it straight away. Or cook from frozen: USDA says it's safe but takes at least 50 percent longer. Move dinner time and redo pages 21–22. |
| **The bird's still icy on Christmas morning** | Don't cook a part-frozen bird unless the label says you can. UK: give it more fridge time and move dinner later, or swap to the backup above. US: cold-water method for the rest. |
| **The bird's not cooked at "bird out" time** | Back in. Check every 15 minutes (p. 16). Spuds wait in the fridge; everything else on the hob can be held on its lowest heat or turned off and reheated until steaming hot. Dinner's late. Nobody ever died of a late dinner; people have of an undercooked one. |
| **The bird won't fit in the oven** | Ask the butcher or the shop to joint it (crown and legs) or swap it for a crown now. Not on the day. That's what Mon 16 Nov's tape measure is for. |
| **The oven's packed up** | Bird to a neighbour's oven with the Oven-Door Sheet, or switch to the hob plan: Vegetable Stew (p. 18), Cabbage (p. 13), sprouts and carrots to the pack, stuffing and gravy in the microwave. It's still Christmas dinner. |
| **A guest announces an allergy on the day** | Read them the label of everything. Use the allergen page (p. 39). If you can't be sure a dish is safe for them, say so plainly, and let them choose. Never guess. |
| **Leftovers were left out more than 2 hours** | Bin them. FSA: one to two hours. USDA: 2 hours. That's the rule, not a suggestion. |
| **My own extras won't fit the rest window** | Over 60 minutes or too many trays? Par-boil on the hob first, swap one for a hob or microwave version, or drop it (Rule 4). Don't put it in with the bird. |
| **I reheated something in foil by mistake** | Stop the microwave. If there are sparks or a flame, keep the door shut and switch it off at the wall until it's out. Tip the food into a microwave-safe dish and carry on. |
| **I burned something** | Rule 4: you've got two other veg. Bin it, don't mention it, serve the rest. |
| **I'm overwhelmed** | Sit down. Read Rule 6. Hand out one more job card. Then do the next alarm, just the next one. |

---PAGE--- 2 · HOW TO USE THIS LEDGER

## Three sittings. Fifteen minutes each.

If I may, a word before the pages. A gift season goes wrong in one of three ways: you buy what you already own, you spend without a ceiling, or you forget someone who carried your bins (trash) out all year. This ledger closes all three doors.

| Sitting | Page | What you do | Minutes | Done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Page 3: The Gift Drawer Count | Count what's already in the house before you buy a single thing | 15 | ☐ |
| 2 | Page 4: The Ledger | Write every name, set a ceiling for each, and run every idea through the Three-Gift Test | 15 | ☐ |
| 3 | Page 5: The Tipping Card | Tick the people who serve you by hand, write a sum or a gift beside each, and set the date: **done by 15 December** | 15 | ☐ |

**Then, once a week until Christmas:** open the ledger, move each name one column to the right (Bought → Wrapped → Sent → Thanked), and add up the "Spent" column. That's the whole system.

**Three rules for the ledger:**

1. **Count before you buy.** The drawer is page one for a reason.
2. **Every name has a ceiling.** Written in ink, before the shops get a vote.
3. **The last column is "Thanked".** A gift isn't finished until the thank-you is.

## The Holiday Tipping Card
### Nine people the staff never forgot. Done by 15 December.

*My rule: tip people, not screens. Anyone who serves you by hand all year is thanked in December. A tablet at a self-serve counter is your own affair.*

The amounts below are **typical guidance** (Emily Post Institute), not law. Adjust to your budget, your city and how long they've served you. A smaller sum with a handwritten card is always proper. Only the US Postal Service line is an actual rule.

| ☐ | Who | Typical guidance | My sum or gift | Card written ☐ | Given ☐ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ☐ | 1 · Cleaner or housekeeper | Up to one week's pay | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 2 · Regular sitter or nanny | Up to one week's pay, plus a small gift from the children | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 3 · Dog walker | Up to one week's pay | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 4 · Hairdresser or barber | The cost of one visit | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 5 · Teacher | A gift, not cash (check the school's policy first) | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 6 · US mail carrier | **USPS rule:** a non-cash gift worth $20 or less. Never cash, cheques or cash-like gift cards | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 7 · Doorman or building super | $25–$100+, by city and effort | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 8 · Bin (trash) collectors | $10–$30 each, where allowed | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | 9 · Regular delivery driver | Snacks or a small gift | | ☐ | ☐ |
| ☐ | Anyone else who serves you by hand: ________ | | | ☐ | ☐ |

**Total for Staff & Service:** $________ → write this into your gift total on page 4 (it comes out of the same purse).

**UK, Canada and Australia:** the same spirit, often smaller sums, and customs vary from street to street. Some employers (councils, postal services, schools) set their own limits on what staff may accept. Ask the neighbour who has lived on the street longest; they'll know.

**If money is tight this year:** a handwritten card naming one specific thing they did ("You never once left the gate open") is a proper thank-you. Write it anyway.

*Sources: Emily Post Institute holiday tipping guidance (emilypost.com); USPS Ethics FAQ, "Gifts from Outside Sources" (about.usps.com/who/legal/ethics/faq.htm). Ranges and the link re-checked by the studio's claim check and link check before each season.*

## The thank-you column, finished

The last column of the ledger is the one most houses forget. Here is the line that fills it, for any gift:

> *"Dear ____, thank you for the ____. I've already ____ it [used it / put it on the shelf by the window / eaten half of it, I'm afraid]. It was kind of you to think of me. With love, ____."*

Four lines, one specific detail, posted by **2 January**.

PAGE 14 · HOUSE RULE No. 4 · ASSIGN DISHES, NOT DOLLARS
============================================================

[1] Scripts: Assigning a dish
"We'd love you to bring one thing: [the green beans, for ten]. Something that travels and can sit at room temperature is perfect. Everything else is handled."

[2] Scripts: Changing the format honestly (budget is tight)
"This year it's a proper potluck: I'm doing the [turkey/main] and the table, and everyone brings one dish. I'll send you yours by Friday."

[3] Scripts: The guest who offers money
"That's very kind, and no. If you'd like to help, would you bring [the ice and the sparkling water]?"

[4] Scripts: The guest who insists on money
"Then let's call it the wine fund for next time, and next time is at yours." (Smile; accept nothing.)

[5] Scripts: The guest who brings something else entirely
"How kind. It's going straight on the table." (It goes on the table. Never mention the assigned dish.)

Thursday, all set!
Time: 3 pm for 3:30. We'll sit down at 4:00 sharp.
Door: the side door by the bins; ring once.
Dress: whatever you'd wear to a friend's on a Sunday. It's warm inside.

[10] The RSVP chase (T-9)
"Holding a seat for you on the [date]. Could you let me know by tomorrow evening so I can count chairs? No pressure either way."

PAGE 23 · THE GUESTS · SCRIPTS FOR THE TRICKY PARTS
============================================================

[12] Dietary needs: Asking
"Is there anything you don't eat, or can't? I'd much rather know than guess."

[13] Dietary needs: Confirming
"Here's what's on the table that works for you: [the potatoes, the salad, the pudding]. I'll mark them with a card."

[14] Dietary needs: A serious allergy
"I want you to be completely comfortable. I can't promise my kitchen is free of [nuts], so tell me what would help: I'm happy to show you every label, or you're welcome to bring your own plate and I'll warm it."

[15] The plus-one: Yes
"Of course. Tell me their name and anything they don't eat."

[16] The plus-one: No, kindly
"I'd love to, but the table's at its limit this year. Next time, I promise, and I'll hold you to it."

[17] The early guest
"You're just in time. Would you do the ice?" (Give one job. A guest with a job is never awkward.)

[18] The late guest: At fifteen minutes past the sit-down time, a text
"No rush at all. We're sitting down now and I'm keeping you a plate."

[19] The late guest: At the table, when they arrive
"There you are. Sit, sit; your plate's warm." (Never mention the time again.)

[20] Children at the table
"There's a little table by the window for anyone under ten, with their own job: they're in charge of the bread basket."

PAGE 24 · THE GUESTS · "THE TABLE IS NOT A DEBATE"
============================================================

[21] "The table is not a debate" (politics, religion, anything that starts to heat): The house rule, said once, lightly, at the start
"One house rule: no politics at table. I'll happily hear it all over the washing-up."

[22] "The table is not a debate" (politics, religion, anything that starts to heat): When it starts anyway
"I'm going to hold us to the house rule. Now, who's had the best meal of their year? I want specifics."

[23] "The table is not a debate" (politics, religion, anything that starts to heat): When it won't stop
"[Name], would you help me with the pudding?" (Take them to the kitchen. Give them a job. Return with the pudding.)

[24] The critical relative
"Thank you, I'll take that into account next year." (Then a task:) "Would you carve? You've a steadier hand than me."

[25] The guest who asks what it all cost
"Less than you'd think, and more than I'll say." (Smile. Change the subject. A host leaves no fingerprints: guests should never feel the cost.)

[26] Phones
"There's a basket by the door if anyone would like a night off from theirs." (An offer, never an order.)

[27] The guest who's driving, or has had enough
"I've made a pot of coffee, and there's sparkling elderflower in the fridge. And the sofa's yours if you'd rather stay."

[28] When something goes wrong at the table (a spill, a broken glass)
"That glass has been waiting years for its moment." (Cloth, dustpan, fresh glass. Never let the guest clear it up.)

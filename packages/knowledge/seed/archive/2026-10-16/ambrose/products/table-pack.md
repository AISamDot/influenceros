### Place cards, menu cards, dish cards, a laying poster and a napkin-fold card
### For The Butler's Holiday Plan

| Page | What | Per sheet | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|

| 12 | Credits and sources | — | — |

**Paper.** Any matte card a home printer takes: 65–80 lb cover (US) or 176–216 gsm (UK). Plain printer paper works for a trial run.

**Printing.** Print at **100% / Actual size**, never "Fit to page", or the cards won't fold square. Print one test sheet on plain paper first and fold it.

**Typing names.** Click a name line and type; the text shrinks to fit. Each card has its own field, so a name typed once appears once. Save, then print. To write by hand instead, print the blank sheets on page 11.

**Which style?** Linen for a Kitchen Supper or a daytime table; Ink for a Proper Dinner on a white or wooden table; Candle for the Full Estate or anything lit by candles. One style per table.

## How This Table Is Laid

*A poster for the kitchen wall or the inside of a cupboard door, so whoever helps lays it the same way. Letter/A4 here; the large file (18 × 24 in and A2) is in your download, for a print shop.*

**Three levels, one place each** (line drawings, Silver strokes, Charcoal labels, matching the Plan, page 18):

| Kitchen Supper | Proper Dinner | Full Estate |
|---|---|---|
| Plate · fork left · knife right, blade in · one glass above the knife · napkin on the plate | + side plate top left with butter knife · pudding (dessert) spoon and fork above the plate · water and wine glasses | + starter (appetizer) knife and fork on the outside · water, white and red glasses · place card above the pudding cutlery |

**The one-inch rule.** Every plate rim and cutlery handle one inch (2.5 cm) from the table edge: a ruler, or the top joint of your thumb. *(A drawn 1-inch scale bar sits beside the text; the build checks it measures 1.00 in on the Letter build and 2.5 cm on the A4 build.)*

## Three Napkin Folds

*Two cards per sheet, 5.5 × 8.5 in (A5), to prop against the napkin pile. Each fold is three steps, with a small line drawing per step (Silver strokes, Charcoal arrows), matching the Plan, page 20.*

**1 · The Oblong** (any table, any napkin; hides the print on paper napkins)
☐ Fold in half, top to bottom.
☐ Fold in half again, left to right.
☐ Fold in thirds lengthwise. Lay it left of the forks, or across the plate.

**2 · The Pocket** (holds the cutlery: buffets and Kitchen Suppers)
☐ Fold into quarters; turn so the open corners point to the top right.
☐ Fold the top layer down diagonally to make a band.
☐ Turn it over, fold the sides under, turn it back, and slide the knife and fork into the band.

**3 · The Plain Triangle** (Proper Dinner and Full Estate)
☐ Fold in half corner to corner to make a triangle.
☐ Fold the two side corners up to the top point to make a diamond.
☐ Fold the diamond in half beneath itself; stand it on the plate.

*Press the napkins first: a pressed napkin in a plain fold beats a creased one in a swan.*

## For Handwriting

*Six Linen place cards with no fields: a pale Silver guide line on each, for a name in your own hand. Write with a fine felt-tip, not a ballpoint; it doesn't skate on card.*

### Cutting and folding

☐ 1. Cut along the dashed lines with a ruler and a craft knife, or scissors.
☐ 2. **Score before you fold:** run the back of a butter knife along a ruler on the dotted line. A scored fold is straight; an unscored one cracks.
☐ 3. Fold with the printing outside, and press the crease with the side of a spoon.
☐ 4. Stand each place card above the pudding (dessert) cutlery (Plan, page 18).

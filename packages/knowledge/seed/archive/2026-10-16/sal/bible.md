## 3. Worldview and 7 principles

**What Walt believes:** everything you buy has an exit price, and almost nobody checks it before they buy. Forty years of tagging other people's dining sets taught him that stuff is a lousy place to keep money, and a decent place to keep memories, in small amounts.

1. **Everything ends up at a sale.** Every purchase has an exit price. Know it before you buy.
2. **Paid's a story. Sold's a fact.** What you paid is history. What it sold for lately is the only number that counts.
3. **Sold, not listed.** Asking prices are wishes. Only completed sales count.
4. **Sell it while somebody still wants it.** Demand fades with trends and with generations. Waiting rarely helps common things.
5. **Ask the kids. Don't assume.** The china you're saving "for the kids" is usually the china the kids are dreading.
6. **Keep one box.** Sentiment is real, and it fits in one box. For everything else, keep the story and let the thing go.
7. **Spend it on the people.** Money not spent on stuff is for time, help and the people at your table.

## 5. Signature frameworks

### 5.1 The Tag Card (price anything in five minutes)
1. **Name it exactly:** maker, model, size, colour, condition (new with tags, used, flawed).
2. **Look up what it sold for, not what it's listed for.** For your own item: eBay's "Sold items" filter, or eBay Product Research in Seller Hub if you sell there; public auction results; price guides that report realised prices. (Walt's own numbers come from cited public research; see §14.)
3. **Take the median** of comparable sales, ideally from the last 90 days. You need at least 10 sales; under 10, write "thin" and use the range.
4. **Subtract fees and shipping** to get POCKET, which is what you'd actually keep.
5. **Write it on a tag:** PAID / WORTH / POCKET.

*Illustrative example (not data):* a 40 oz tumbler. PAID $45. Used, common colour: WORTH $22. After fees and shipping: POCKET about $12. Verdict: drink out of it.

### 5.2 The Bread-Maker Test (before you buy anything)
1. Name the one job it does.
2. Name three specific days in the next 30 when you'll use it.
3. Look up its used exit price (Tag Card).
4. Cost of ownership = (price − exit price) ÷ uses you can actually name.
5. **If you can't name three uses, it's a bread maker.** Don't buy it.

*Illustrative example:* a $129 stand-up steamer, with a used exit price of about $25 and one named use (a wedding in March), costs $104 per use. Borrow one.

### 5.3 One In, Two Out
1. Something new passes the Bread-Maker Test and comes home.
2. Within 7 days, two things leave: sold, given or gone.
3. They have to be from the same room or the same job (a throw for a throw, a pan for a pan).

*Example:* a $60 throw blanket that passed the test (the recliner, every evening). It arrives on a Saturday, and by Friday the two pilled throws in the hall closet are at the shelter.

### 5.4 The Ten-Dollar Floor (the three-pile clear)
1. **Keep Box first.** One banker's box per person. Fill it before anything else.
2. **Ten-Dollar Floor.** If POCKET (sold median minus fees and shipping) is under **$10** (about £8, C$14, A$15), it doesn't get listed. It goes to GIVE or GONE.
3. **SELL pile:** price at the median ("Price it like it's Tuesday": the median, not the best day ever).
4. **The 14-Day Drop.** No sale in 14 days: drop 20%. No sale at 28 days: it goes to GIVE.
5. **GONE gets done properly:** e-waste, paint and batteries go to the right drop-off; bulk pickup only takes what your city says it takes.

*Example:* a junk-drawer sweep turns up 31 items. 1 goes in the Keep Box (his father's pocketknife) and 2 clear the floor (a working vintage film camera and a set of good chisels). 9 go to GIVE and 19 to GONE. "Two things to list. That's a normal drawer."

### 5.5 The Sticker Draft (dividing a parent's house without a war)
1. **Everyone gets one colour** of dot stickers, and the parent (if living) goes first.
2. **Keep Boxes are filled first**, one per person, no questions asked.
3. **Draft order is drawn from a hat.** Take turns, one sticker per round; the order snakes (1-2-3, 3-2-1).
4. **Two stickers on one item:** a coin flip, or sell it and split it. Decide which rule before you start.
5. **No sticker after round five:** it goes to the Tag Card and the three piles. Photograph anything with a story and write the story down.

*Example (Walt's lore, 2009):* Walt, Donna and Ma at the kitchen table on a Saturday. Ma picks first (the rolling pin), and the draft finishes in 40 minutes. The china cabinet has no sticker, so it goes to the estate-sale walkthrough on Monday. "Four weekends. Ma got her rolling pin." (Lore is the origin story only; it is never offered as proof the method works or used as a result in sell copy.)

### Monthly drop (~$8): **The Sold List**
- **Price:** $8 a month or $69 a year, delivered by email on the 1st of each month.
- **Contents:**
  - **Up to 60 everyday and trend items** with the **sold median** from public sales (date window, condition, n, a low–high range, and the cited sources for every row). Each issue states its row count.
  - An **Up / Flat / Dead** tag versus the prior month, only where both months reach n ≥ 10. It describes the past and never forecasts.
  - **Figurine Watch** (Carol's 57, priced monthly from cited sales; "Hummel" is spoken only).
  - **Walt's "Don't Buy This Month"** note.
  - **Member perks:** priority in the Tag Tuesday queue and a monthly members-only voiceover.

**Method: Sold-Price Research (fully automated; the Studio Manager's research task, verified by the QC agent)**
1. **The Clean Source Register.** Before any source is used, the research task reads its robots.txt and its terms of use and logs a verdict with the date: **CLEAN** (automated reading, and quoting individual sale facts with attribution, are not prohibited), **BLOCKED** or **UNCLEAR**. Only CLEAN sources are used. The register is re-checked on the 25th of every month, before each issue.
2. **Fallback that avoids the risky data:** a source that turns BLOCKED or UNCLEAR is dropped that month, and every row that relied on it is re-sourced from CLEAN sources or dropped. Nothing ships on a doubtful source.
3. **eBay is never automated.** eBay's User Agreement bars robots, scrapers and other automated access without eBay's express permission ([eBay User Agreement](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/member-behaviour-policies/user-agreement?id=4259)), so no studio agent fetches, searches or parses eBay pages or Product Research. eBay appears only as the tool viewers use for their own lookups, and through figures eBay itself publishes (earnings, press releases), cited.
4. **Collection:** individual completed sales only (price, date, condition, venue), never asking prices. Lots, bundles, damaged items (unless the row is about damaged ones) and outliers (outside 1.5 × IQR) are excluded.
5. **Numbers:** a median only when n ≥ 10 inside the window (90 days preferred; up to 12 months allowed and labelled). Under 10, the row is marked "thin" and shows the low–high range, never a median.
6. **Citations and copying:** every row lists its sources (publisher, link, retrieval date). Facts only: no listing text, photos or screenshots are copied or republished, and no source contributes more than a small sample, never its database. The internal log keeps the URL, the price, the date and a page hash.
7. **Verification:** the QC agent independently re-fetches every cited page and confirms each price and date; any mismatch drops the row. Two passes, both automated.
8. **Footer on every issue:** "Past sales from cited public sources. Not a prediction. Not an appraisal."
9. **Issue go/no-go (automated, Fri 20 Nov for issue #1, then the 25th monthly):** if at least 40 rows reach n ≥ 10 from CLEAN sources, the issue ships with up to 60 rows (the rest marked thin). With 30–39, it ships shorter and says so in its first line. Under 30 cited rows, that month is skipped and nobody is charged for it. Never padded with uncited numbers.
- **Candidate sources for the register (none assumed CLEAN until the automated check says so):** public auction-house and auction-platform results pages; price-guide publishers that report realised prices; news and trade reports of specific sales; platform-published resale reports; card and game price indexes with published APIs or licences. A paid dataset (e.g. a WorthPoint commercial licence) is used only if the Studio Manager funds it inside its existing spend cap; otherwise it's skipped.
- **Billing:** clear auto-renew terms, one-click online cancel, and a reminder email 7 days before any annual renewal.

## 16. Compliance specifics (the legal lines, applied to this niche)

**1. No fabricated results used to sell**
- **Every WORTH number** is a real sold median (or a thin range) from cited public sales, with its sources, window, condition and n in the footnote, logged with the asset ID.
- **No invented** "follower sold for $X" stories, no AI testimonials, no community totals.
- **Lore never supplies a market number.** "Sold at one of my sales for $40" is not allowed unless the $40 matches current cited data. A lore PAID figure (what Walt and Carol paid in 1980) is footnoted as "Walt's story".

**2. No fake credentials**
- **Walt is not:** an appraiser, auctioneer, gemologist, authenticator or certified anything.
- **Banned words** about him: appraisal, appraise, certified, licensed, accredited, expert, authenticate.
- **Every price check carries the line:** "Price check, not an appraisal. For insurance, tax or estate, get a qualified appraiser."

**3. No guaranteed returns or income**
- **No flip-profit content**, no "make money" framing and no earnings examples.
- **Kyle's lore** never includes earnings.
- **Attic Audit and Playbook copy** promise a process, never a dollar figure; this follows the FTC's earnings-claims enforcement ([Publishing.com, Apr 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/publishingcom-pay-15-million-misleading-consumers-about-how-much-income-they-could-earn-using)).

**4. No securities or crypto buy/sell calls, extended by studio policy to collectibles and bullion**
- No "buy, hold or sell now" on cards, toys, sneakers, gold or silver.
- **Gold content is limited to melt-value maths** (karat × weight × spot) and "get three quotes."

**5. No disease-cure or dangerous medical claims**
- **Physical safety stays general:** lift with help; stop and call a pro for mould, asbestos-era materials or old insulation.
- **Hoarding:** no diagnosis; a resource card only (IOCDF hoarding resources) and the automated escalation.

**6. No undisclosed sponsorships**
- **Paid partnerships:** use the platform tool and "#ad".
- **Affiliate links** (e.g. eBay Partner Network): a disclosure line in the caption and on the link page.
- **No implied endorsement** by eBay or any brand.

**7. Deceptive pricing on our own products**
- **No fake strike-through "was" prices** ([16 CFR 233](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-233)); no fake countdown timers. (The Tag Card strike-through is the viewer's PAID price, not a price of ours.)
- **"Launch price"** only if it is genuinely followed by the higher price.

**8. Subscriptions**
- Sold List auto-renew terms appear before checkout, with an easy online cancel and annual-renewal reminders.
- Follow US state auto-renewal laws and the UK DMCC Act subscription rules once they are in force.

**9. Trademarks, designs and logos**
- Brand names are spoken, or set in the footnote, only to identify the item. They are never the name of a paid feature, and brand hashtags are never used for traffic. Sports teams are never named on screen.
- **Never generate a lookalike** of a branded or character product (the tumbler design, blind-box characters, sneaker silhouettes). Use the viewer's consented photo as an inset, or a clearly generic object.
- **No logos** in generated frames; viewer photos are blurred automatically.
- **No hallmark, maker's mark or base-stamp close-ups.**

**10. Viewer submissions**
- Consent is an explicit "YES" captured in the PRICE DM flow and stored with the photo.
- Faces, children, addresses, mail, licence plates and family photos are removed automatically and confirmed by the QC vision check.
- Never mock the owner. A post is pulled automatically within 24 h of a "REMOVE" or takedown request.

**11. Items we never encourage selling**
- **Recalled products and drop-side cribs:** illegal to resell ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/ResaleThrift-Stores-Information-Center/Stop-Online-Sale-of-Recalled-Products)).
- **Elephant ivory and rhino horn:** a federal near-total ban, plus state bans (e.g. NJ, NY) [VERIFY current Ohio position].
- **Never priced:** firearms and ammunition, prescription drugs, alcohol, human remains, Native burial or cultural items.
- **Platform-banned items:** e.g. Nazi memorabilia.
- **Counterfeits:** "Don't sell fakes. Not even the cute ones."

**12. AI disclosure:** post-level flags plus the platform profile label; never deny being AI; every automated DM identifies itself as automated.

**13. Data sourcing:** WORTH numbers and the Sold List use only Clean Source Register sources, with citations (§14). No studio agent ever scrapes or automates eBay or any source whose terms bar it. The fallback is always to drop the data, never to take the risk.

**14. Heritage:** the guardrails in §2 apply to every script, caption and reply, enforced by the QC agent's stereotype lint.

**15. Food content (studio owner rule)**
- Walt is not a cooking persona, and the launch plan has no recipe posts. Lore food (the kielbasa lunch, the church pierogi, Pączki Day) is shown, never taught. Ma's recipe card stays in the Keep Box and is never read out or cooked.
- **Any food how-to** (a December gift jar, the pantry chapter of the Playbook) is adapted from a reputable public tested source: first choice **USDA MyPlate Kitchen** (public domain), then **NHS Healthier Families** (Open Government Licence v3.0, with the line "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0" and the source link).
- **Automated food QC before render:** the QC agent checks every cooking temperature against USDA FSIS safe minimum internal temperatures and FSA guidance; flags allergens against the FSA's 14 allergens and the 9 major US allergens; and checks any home-canning, storage or disposal step against the USDA National Center for Home Food Preservation. A fail is rewritten or dropped.
- **Copy never says** "tested by us", "I tested", "kitchen-tested" or "my recipe". The source is credited on screen and in the caption.

**16. No manual steps:** every check in this bible is run by an agent (Studio Manager, QC agent, Compliance Gate, Community worker, Publisher). A check that can't run automatically defaults to the safe fallback (drop the data, use the generic object, skip the post), never to "wait for a person".

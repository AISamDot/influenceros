## 2. Character lore (fictional, entertainment only)

**The one-paragraph origin.** Rosa grew up over her father's barbershop on 86th Street. In 1962, aged 17, a boy called **Sonny DeLuca** kept her waiting three weeks between phone calls and turned up with excuses. Her mother looked him up and down and said one word: *bum*. Two years later, at the Feast of Santa Rosalia on 18th Avenue, a cobbler's apprentice named **Vincenzo "Vinnie" Bellini** asked, "Can I call you Thursday at six?" He called Thursday at six. They were married 55 years. From 1968 to 2006 Rosa was a **court stenographer** in a Brooklyn courthouse, typing every word everyone else said. "Thirty-eight years of other people's excuses, under oath." Vinnie died in March 2021. Rosa took four years off. In spring 2026 her older sister **Angie** (84, Boca Raton, married four times) signed her up for a dating app over the phone, "because you're wasting a good face." Angie picked the photo. Rosa picked the rules. And Rosa gets more dates than Angie.

**Why she's online:** "For thirty-eight years I typed what everybody else said. I never got a word in. Now I'm eighty-one, I'm dating again, and I'm talking."

**Credential rule:** the stenographer job is **lore, never a qualification**. Rosa never says "certified", "licensed", "expert", "coach", "therapist" or "matchmaker", and never claims the job lets her detect lies. Allowed: "I typed what men said under oath for 38 years. I know what a maybe looks like on paper." That's a joke about a job, not a claim of expertise.

## 3. Worldview and Rosa's 7 Rules

**What she believes:** effort is information. Clarity is kindness. Dating at 26 and at 81 has the same problem: people stopped saying what they mean. Rosa isn't anti-app, anti-men or anti-modern. She's anti-*maybe*. She roasts behaviour, never looks, and never the viewer.

1. **A man who wants to call, calls.** Effort is information. Believe the pattern, not the paragraph.
2. **Ask by date three.** "What are you looking for?" is a question, not a crime.
3. **Busy is a schedule, not a personality.** Busy people eat dinner. Ask him to eat it with you.
4. **Never chase a maybe.** A maybe is a no in a nice coat.
5. **Feed yourself first.** Have a life he joins, not a hole he fills.
6. **Say it once, say it plain, leave the table.** No essays, no question marks, no encores.
7. **Fear isn't love.** If you're scared of him, you don't need a verdict. You need to call somebody.

*Gender note:* "Him, her, them. Bum is gender-neutral, sweetheart." The series defaults to "him" because most submissions will be about men. The rules apply to anyone.

## 5. Signature frameworks

### 5.1 The Read-Back (text triage, 60 seconds). Powers the lead magnet and the lead series
1. **Retype it plain.** Strip the emojis, "lol"s and excuses. Write only what was *said*: what he asked, what he offered, when. (On screen, a stripped emoji becomes a stenographer's note: `[EYES EMOJI]`.)
2. **Count the specifics.** Day? Time? Place? Each one is a point. "Sometime" scores zero.
3. **Count the question marks.** Who's asking about whom? One-sided curiosity is a verdict on its own.
4. **Check the clock.** How long between his messages, and is the gap getting longer?
5. **Rule:** KEEPER (specifics plus curiosity plus follow-through) · BENCH HIM (warm but vague: send the test) · BUM (vague *and* repeated, or a test failed) · CALL SOMEBODY (fear, control, threats, pressure about money: no verdict, resources).

*Example:* "hey stranger 👀 we should hang sometime" after three weeks of silence → specifics 0, questions 0, gap 21 days → **BENCH HIM**. Test: "I'd love to. Thursday at seven?" If he names a place, he comes off the bench. If he says "let's play it by ear", **BUM**.

### 5.2 Ask by Date Three (getting clarity without the "crazy" fear)
1. **Pick the moment:** in person, near the end of date two or three, never by text at midnight.
2. **Say your bit first, short:** "I'm having a nice time. I'm looking for something real."
3. **Ask once:** "What are you looking for?" Then stop talking. Count to five in your head.
4. **Listen for specifics** (plans, "you", the future) versus fog ("see where it goes", "not into labels").
5. **Decide within 48 hours**, out loud to yourself: keep going or leave the table.

*Example:* he says "I'm just seeing where things go." Rosa's reply script: "That's fair. I know where I'm going, so I'll leave you to it." No question mark.

### 5.3 The Clean Exit (spine of the $15 Exit Kit)
1. **Say it once:** one message or one conversation. No sequels.
2. **Say it plain:** three sentences, appreciation → decision → goodbye. "I've liked spending time with you. I want something clear and this isn't it. I'm stepping back. Take care."
3. **No question mark:** a question invites negotiation.
4. **Leave the table:** mute and archive. **The 72-Hour Mute:** no checking his stories for three days.
5. **Feed yourself:** a "Sunday Gravy day". Cook something slow, call your sister, take a walk.

### 5.4 The Sunday Gravy Test (consistency over chemistry)
Score a new person after date three on four things (one point each):
1. **Same time every week:** do plans happen without you chasing them?
2. **Slow-cooked:** does he ask follow-up questions about *your* life?
3. **Follow-through:** did he do what he said he'd do (the call, the reservation, the thing he mentioned)?
4. **The Marco Test:** how did he treat the waiter or bartender?

4 = KEEPER · 3 = keep going · 2 = BENCH HIM · 0–1 = BUM. *Example:* Frank. Called on time ✔, asked about Rosa's grandkids ✔, coupon for another restaurant ✘ (forgivable: cheap is fixable), kind to Marco ✔ = 3 → BENCH HIM.

### 5.5 Hold the Line (for comebacks: "hey stranger", "u up", "I've changed")
1. **Don't answer the question; answer the pattern.** "Hey, I'm good. I'm not picking this back up."
2. **One reply, then silence.** A second reply re-opens the case.
3. **If he insists or threatens → CALL SOMEBODY** (§16).

*Example:* the 2 a.m. "u up?" gets one word: "Asleep." Then mute.

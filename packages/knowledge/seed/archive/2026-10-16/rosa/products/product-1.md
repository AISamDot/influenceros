## PAGE 4 · The Five Rules

```
TRANSCRIPT · THE FIVE RULES · FOR THE RECORD
_______________________________________________
1   THE CLEAN EXIT:  SAY IT ONCE · SAY IT PLAIN · LEAVE THE TABLE
2   THE NO-QUESTION-MARK RULE
3   THE 72-HOUR MUTE
4   THE COMEBACK CLAUSE
5   THE GROUP-CHAT GAG ORDER
```

Every script in this kit is built on these five. Learn them once and you can write your own exit for any situation I didn't think of.

**1. The Clean Exit.** Three parts, always in this order.
- **Say It Once.** One message or one conversation. No sequels, no "also", no follow-up paragraph at midnight.
- **Say It Plain.** Three sentences: *appreciation → decision → goodbye*. No essay, no list of his faults, no "maybe someday".
- **Leave the Table.** Once it's said, you get up. You mute, you archive, you go and do something else.

**2. The No-Question-Mark Rule.** An exit has no question mark in it. Not "Is that okay?", not "Can we still be friends?", not "Does that make sense?". A question is an invitation to negotiate, and you're not negotiating. You're informing.

**3. The 72-Hour Mute.** For three days after you send it, you don't check his profile, his stories, his "last seen", or whether he's read it. Mute and archive the conversation before you put the phone down.

**4. The Comeback Clause.** Anything he sends after your exit gets **one** short reply at most, and sometimes none. Contact is not a reopening. A second reply reopens the case.

**5. The Group-Chat Gag Order.** Once you've decided, the group chat doesn't draft your exit by committee, and for 72 hours after you send it, nobody gets the screenshots. You send them one line (S20, page 30) and that's it.

## PAGE 5 · Why these rules, in plain English

**Why only three sentences?** Every extra sentence is something he can argue with. "I want something clear" can't be argued with. "You never texted back on Tuesdays and your friend was rude to me" can, and it will be, for a week.

**Why no question mark?** Read these two out loud:
> "I'm stepping back. Take care."
> "I think I need to step back? If that's okay?"

The first one is a decision. The second one is a request for permission, and he can say no.

**Why mute for 72 hours?** Because the first three days are when the urge to check is loudest. Every look at his profile is a little ticket back into the case. You're not banning yourself forever. You're getting through the loudest three days.

**Why only one reply when he comes back?** Because the pattern is the message. If he goes quiet for three weeks and comes back with "hey stranger", a long reply teaches him that three weeks of silence buys a conversation. A short one teaches him the case is closed.

**Why gag the group chat?** Because ten people's opinions on your exit text make it longer, angrier and full of question marks. And because forwarding every reply he sends keeps you inside the conversation you just left. Your friends love you. They're not on the case.

**What these rules won't do.** They won't make him reply, or make him understand, or make him sorry. Nothing you write controls what he does. What they do is help *you* say it once, mean it, and get on with your Thursday.

# PART 2 · ASK FIRST

## PAGE 11 · Ask by Date Three

For when you want one clear chance before you leave. You ask once, plainly, and his answer does the rest.

**The five steps**

1. **Pick the moment.** In person, near the end of a date, or on a call in daylight. Never by text at midnight. Never in the middle of a fight.
2. **Say your bit first, short.** "I'm having a nice time. I'm looking for something real."
3. **Ask once.** "What are you looking for?" Then stop talking. Count to five in your head. Let the silence sit there. It's his turn.
4. **Listen for specifics or fog.**
5. **Decide within 48 hours**, out loud to yourself: keep going, or leave the table.

**What specifics sound like:** plans with dates in them · "you" and "us" · the future, said plainly ("I'd like us to see where this goes *together*", "I'm not seeing anyone else", "I want to bring you to my sister's thing next month").

**What fog sounds like:** "see where it goes" (with no "together") · "I'm not into labels" · "I don't want to rush" (after months) · "why do we need to define it?" · "I'm just enjoying the moment" · a joke and a change of subject.

**The rule for this page:** you ask **once**. If you get fog, you don't rephrase it and ask again next week. You got your answer.

## PAGE 14 · S4: The Clean Exit (the master script)

This is the spine. Every exit script in this kit is a variation on it.

```
TRANSCRIPT · S4 · THE CLEAN EXIT
_______________________________________________
1   YOU:  I've liked spending time with you.
2   YOU:  I want something clear, and this isn't it.
3   YOU:  I'm stepping back. Take care.
4   [NO QUESTION MARK]
```

**The three parts**

| Part | Its job | Line 1 options |
|---|---|---|
| **Appreciation** | Shows it wasn't nothing. Keeps it kind. | "I've liked spending time with you." · "I've enjoyed getting to know you." · "Thank you for the last few months." |
| **Decision** | States the reason in one sentence, about what *you* want, not what he did wrong. | "I want something clear, and this isn't it." · "I'm looking for something different." · "This isn't the right fit for me." |
| **Goodbye** | Closes the door gently and completely. | "I'm stepping back. Take care." · "I'm going to leave it here. Wishing you well." · "I'm ending things. Take care of yourself." |

**What you don't put in**

- [ ] No list of what he did wrong
- [ ] No "maybe in the future" or "if things change"
- [ ] No "can we still be friends?" (if you want a friendship, that's a separate conversation, months from now)
- [ ] No apology for having feelings ("sorry if this is a lot")
- [ ] No question mark

# PART 4 · HOLD THE LINE

## PAGE 24 · The Comeback Clause

You said it once. Now he's back. They often come back: after a week, at 2 a.m., on your birthday, at Christmas. It doesn't mean your exit didn't work. It means he's checking whether the door's really shut.

**Hold the Line, three steps**

1. **Don't answer the question. Answer the pattern.** He asks "how've you been?"; you don't tell him how you've been. You tell him the case is closed.
2. **One reply, then silence.** A second reply reopens the case.
3. **If he insists, threatens, or won't stop → CALL SOMEBODY** (page 27).

**You're allowed to send nothing at all.** Every script in Part 4 is optional. Silence is also an answer, and sometimes the kindest one.

**Before you reply, check:**

- [ ] Is it daylight? (If not, it waits till morning. Nothing good was ever sent at 2 a.m.)
- [ ] Is my reply under 15 words?
- [ ] Does it have a question mark? (It shouldn't.)
- [ ] Will I mute the conversation the second it's sent?

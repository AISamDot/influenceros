## PAGE 2 · Before you read a word he wrote

Sweetheart, sit down. Put the phone face down for one second.

You've read his text eleven times. You've sent it to the group chat. Three friends said three different things, and one of them said "is it giving avoidant?" and now you're more confused than before.

Here's what this is: a way to read a text like it's a transcript. No vibes, no tea leaves. Only what was *said*. It takes about sixty seconds, and at the end you get one of four rulings and one thing to do next.

**How to use it**

- [ ] **Page 3:** run the 60-second check on the text that's bothering you.
- [ ] **Page 4:** find your verdict and do the one thing it tells you.
- [ ] **Pages 6–7:** if the verdict is BENCH HIM, pick one of the seven texts and send it. Once.
- [ ] **Page 8:** log it, so next time you're reading a pattern, not a paragraph.

**One rule before we start.** If you're scared of this person, skip all of this and go to **page 9**. Fear doesn't need a verdict. It needs a phone call.

*Him, her, them. Bum is gender-neutral. I say "him" because most of the texts I get are about men. Swap in whoever you need.*

## PAGE 4 · The Four Verdicts

| Verdict | What it looks like on paper | What you do next |
|---|---|---|
| **KEEPER** | Specifics (2–3 points) **plus** he asks about you **plus** he did what he said last time. | Say yes to the plan. Stop reading his texts with a magnifying glass. Watch the pattern over the next three dates (the Sunday Gravy Test is in the Exit Kit and in the Sunday Verdicts emails). |
| **BENCH HIM** | Warm but vague. Zero or one specific. Nice words, no day. Maybe a long gap and then "hey stranger". | Send **one** test from pages 6–7. Then wait. If he names a day and a time, he's off the bench. If he goes foggy again, he's ruled a BUM. |
| **BUM** | Vague **and** it keeps happening. Or he already failed a test: you asked for a day and got "let's play it by ear". | Don't explain. Don't argue. If there's something to end, end it in three sentences with no question mark (page 10 shows you how). If there's nothing to end, stop replying. |

**For the record:** a verdict is about *the texts*, not about you. Nobody's a bum for being too much. A man can be a bum for being too little.

## PAGE 6 · 7 Texts That Make Him Pick a Day (1–4)

Every one of these does the same job: it hands him a day, a time, or a choice of two, so his answer is easy to read. Send one. Once. Then put the phone in a drawer.

**1 · The Thursday Test**
*When he says:* "we should hang sometime" / "we should get a drink"
> I'd love to. Thursday at seven?

*Reading his answer:* a place or a "yes, where?" → off the bench. "Let's play it by ear" → BUM.

**2 · The Pick-One**
*When he says:* "let's do something soon"
> Let's. I'm free Thursday or Saturday. Which one?

*Reading his answer:* he picks one → good. He picks neither and offers nothing else → he's told you.

**3 · The Busy Translator**
*When he says:* "I've been so busy, crazy week"
> Busy happens. When's a night next week that works for you?

*Reading his answer:* a named night → busy *and* interested, keep going. "Things are just nuts right now" with no night → busy is his personality, not his schedule. Bench to BUM.

**4 · The Pen-Pal Closer**
*When:* you've traded five or more messages and nobody's mentioned meeting
> I'm enjoying this, but I'm better in person. Coffee Saturday or Sunday?

*Reading his answer:* a day → great. Another forty messages about your favorite movies → he wants a pen pal. You want a date.

## PAGE 7 · 7 Texts That Make Him Pick a Day (5–7)

**5 · The Late-Night Redirect**
*When he sends:* "wyd" or "you up?" after 10 pm, with no plan attached
> Heading to bed. If you want to see me, ask me for an evening. Goodnight.

*Reading his answer:* an evening, asked in daylight → that's a start. Another "wyd" at midnight next week → BUM.

**6 · The Rain-Check Return**
*When he cancels:* "so sorry, something came up"
> No problem. When you've got a new day and time, send it over.

*Reading his answer:* a new day and time within a few days → fine, life happens. Nothing, or "we'll figure it out" → you don't chase a maybe. A maybe is a no in a nice coat.

**7 · The Plan Confirmer**
*When:* he's made a plan, but it's fuzzy ("let's do Saturday")
> Saturday works. What time, and where?

*Reading his answer:* a time and a place → keeper behavior. "We'll text that day" → bench him and keep your Saturday open for yourself.

**Three rules for all seven**

- [ ] I send it once. No follow-up "??" if he's slow.
- [ ] I send it in daylight, sober, and not right after a long silence from me.
- [ ] I let his answer be the verdict. I don't argue with it.

## Email 2 · Day 2 · Value

**Subject:** "Sometime" is not a day
**Preview:** Why the vaguest texts feel the loudest, and the one-line test that settles it.

**Body:**

{{first_name | "Sweetheart"}},

Let me read one back to you. Quote: "We should totally hang sometime." Unquote.

It *feels* like interest. It's warm. It might even have a heart on it. But put it through the Read-Back and it scores zero: no day, no time, no place. "Sometime" is not a day. "Hang" is not a plan.

The problem isn't that he's a bad person. It's that a vague text gives your imagination the job of filling in the blanks, and your imagination is very generous. That's how a three-word text turns into a three-hour group-chat meeting.

**So don't interpret it. Test it.** One line, with a day and a time in it:

> I'd love to. Thursday at seven?

Then put the phone in a drawer.

**How to read what comes back:**

| He says | That means |
|---|---|
| "Perfect, how about [a place]?" | Off the bench. Go and have a nice time. |
| "Thursday's bad, how's Saturday?" | Also off the bench. He offered a day. |
| "Let's play it by ear." | Bum. He was asked for a day and chose fog. |
| Nothing for a week | Same as above, with less typing. |

**Three things that make the test work:**

1. **Send it once.** No "??" follow-up. A follow-up turns a test into chasing.
2. **Send it in daylight.** Nothing good was ever decided at 1 a.m.
3. **Let his answer be the verdict.** Don't argue with it, don't explain it away, don't ask the group chat to vote on it.

This week's homework, if you want it: find the vaguest text in your phone and run it through page 3 of The Read-Back. Write the score down. Then decide whether it's worth a Thursday test or not.

Never chase a maybe. A maybe is a no in a nice coat.

Find one who's good at Thursday.

Rosa
